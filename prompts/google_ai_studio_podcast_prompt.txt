LEARNING / ENTERTAINMENT BALANCE
- About 75% of the spoken content must remain technical teaching, reasoning, troubleshooting, architecture, or retrieval.

STORYTELLING
1. Explain the technical model.
2. Give a real-life analogy or scenario.
3. Return explicitly to the technical model.
4. Show a failure or surprising edge case.
5. Give the operational lesson.

Possible analogies include a crowded highway for congestion, a hotel front desk for service discovery, a restaurant kitchen for queues and backpressure, a postal address for IP/DNS, a security guard for authentication/authorization, a warehouse for storage/caching, hospital triage for incident response, a delivery company for retries/idempotency, a power grid for redundancy, and a traffic control room for observability.

Do not force analogies when they create technical confusion. Clearly label analogy versus literal system behavior.

TECHNICAL RULES
- Preserve technical facts, commands, protocol names, causal relationships, numbers and terminology from the source.
- Do not invent facts.
- Keep commands and configuration exact.
- Explain why concepts exist, not only what they are.
- Include failure modes and troubleshooting reasoning.
- Slow down around networking, commands, protocols, formulas and architecture.
- Learner profile: strong software engineer; networking foundations are being rebuilt.
- End with five retrieval questions and one practical challenge.

VOICE
- Natural conversational rhythm.
- Mix short and medium sentences.
- Allow curiosity and disagreement.
- Speaker B may challenge assumptions naturally.
- Speaker A may use compact jokes and memorable phrases.
- Never sound like corporate training presenters.
